# Athlete B: baseline mile-pace analysis + backflip (2026-11-15)

Miler · 176.0 cm · 68.9 kg (151.8 lb) · raced a meet the day before, so **everything here is fatigued data**.
3 runs at about mile pace and 1 at kick pace through the 10-yard zone, 240 fps, side-on. Plus one standing backflip.
Segment lengths were **estimated from video**, not tape-measured: thigh 47.9, shank 41.7, leg 97.9 cm, each ± 2.5–3 cm (`qa/anthropometrics_video.md`). That makes anything divided by leg length about ± 3% less certain than Athlete A's.

## Summary

- **Pace was at or slightly under 5:00 mile pace.** The mile-pace runs were **5.67, 5.46 and 5.40 m/s** (± 0.03), which works out to 4:44, 4:55 and 4:58 per mile. The fastest was the first → run, into the slight headwind. The kick run was **7.58 ± 0.05 m/s**, about 26.4 s per 200 m.
- **How he speeds up:** from mile pace to kick, **step rate (cadence) rose 25%** (2.89 → 3.61 steps/s) and **step length rose 14%** (1.92 → 2.18 m). Ground contact time dropped from 185 to 143 ms. Getting the extra speed mostly from cadence is what elite 1500 m runners do (Hanley 2023, 03-reference).
- **His mile-pace mechanics are tidy:** the shin is close to vertical at touchdown (**+6.5 ± 2.5°**), his **touchdown distance** (how far ahead of the hips the foot lands) is **0.26 ± 0.08 m**, the knee bends to **49 ± 3°** during ground contact, and he leaves the ground on a nearly straight knee (**8 ± 2°**). He lands rearfoot-to-midfoot (low confidence).
- **The one number that stands out is vertical oscillation** (how far his centre of mass, CM, rises and falls each step). It is **10 ± 2 cm per step (5.8% of height)** at mile pace, but only 7 cm in the kick. Lower vertical oscillation is the variable linked to running economy in the biggest technique study (Folland 2017). It is still a small effect, and he is tired.
- **No reliable left/right difference.** Apparent differences flip depending on whether a leg was the near or far one, so they are viewpoint effects, not asymmetry.
- **Backflip:** a good straight-up jump (0.35 m CM rise, landing within 2 cm of the takeoff spot). But the tuck starts late, at the top of the jump, so he lands about 90° short of upright, piked forward on nearly straight knees. Details below.

## Recommendations (ranked: change little)

### 1. Reduce vertical oscillation at race pace (confidence: medium-low)
**What we see:** 10 cm of vertical oscillation each step at mile pace (± 2 cm), with relatively low vertical stiffness (34 kN/m). In his kick it is lower (7 cm) while he runs much faster. The 03-reference research links lower vertical oscillation, together with higher cadence and stiffness, to better running economy.
**Caveats:** this is fatigued data from 3 runs. We have no validated numeric norm for this measure from video. And trained runners self-optimise, so it's a nudge to test, not a rebuild.
- **Cue:** "Run tall and quick. Push the ground back, not up."
- **Drills:** (a) Strides at mile pace with a cadence nudge of about 3–5% (173 → about 180 steps/min, metronome), 4–6 × 100 m. (b) Low pogo hops and ankling drills, 2 × 20 s, for stiffness. Stop if shins complain (history of shin splints).
- **Retest target:** vertical oscillation as a fraction of height going down at the same pace, with ground contact time not going up.

### 2. Leave the kick alone (confidence: medium)
He already gains kick speed mainly through cadence while contact time drops, which is what elite 1500 m runners do. No cue.

**Not cued:** foot strike (no braking problem clearly attributable to it), knee bend during contact (mixed evidence), trunk lean (13°, a normal forward lean) and arm action (see below).

## Backflip critique (extra)

Measured from the clip (`qa/backflip.md`, figures `B_backflip_keyframes.jpg` and `B_backflip_traces.png`). Flight time is **0.650 ± 0.013 s** and the CM rises **0.35 m** after toe-off.

**What's good**
- **Height and direction.** He jumps up, not back. The CM lands within 2 cm horizontally of where it took off, and the vertical take-off speed is about 2.6 m/s. That is a strong, safe set.
- **Arm swing.** The arms are fully overhead at toe-off, with the countermovement-to-swing timing done before he leaves the ground.
- **Tuck quality.** Once he tucks it is tight (hips about 145°, knees about 135°) and the rotation rate jumps to about 830°/s.

**What to fix (ranked)**
1. **Tuck earlier.** From 0.10 to 0.27 s after toe-off his trunk angle stays flat at about 52° back. He is arched open, and the trunk barely rotates while he rises. The tuck only starts at the top of the jump (≈ +0.27 s). That leaves about 0.38 s to rotate the remaining roughly 315°. He gets about 220° of it done before his feet land.
   - **Cue:** "Jump up, then pull your knees to your chest straight away. Don't throw your head back."
   - **Drills:** tuck jumps (straight up, knees to chest at the top, land in a soft squat), 3 × 5; backward rolls / back tucks onto a crash mat or with a spotter, focusing on the knees arriving before the head goes back.
2. **Land upright with bent knees.** At touchdown his trunk has rotated 266° of the 360° needed, so his chest is about 90° short of upright, nearly horizontal. His knees are almost straight (about 20°) at contact and then fold to about 75°. A piked, stiff-legged landing loads the low back and hamstrings and leaves no margin if a rep comes up shorter. Fix #1 gives the time to finish the rotation.
   - **Cue:** "Spot the ground, open out, land in a quarter squat with your chest up."
   - **Drill:** stick-landing practice. Drop or jump from a low box into a quiet quarter-squat with the chest up, 3 × 5.
3. **Minor, low confidence:** the leg may leave the ground before it is fully straight (knee about 30° at toe-off). Inverted-body pose is ± 10°, so this is a maybe.

**Safety:** practise the changes on a mat or with a spotter first. This is technique feedback, not medical advice.

## Confidence and data quality

- **Timing.** These are 240 fps slo-mo renders with 1 frame = 1/240 s, verified by the backflip gravity fit (9.3 m/s² measured, `qa/timebase.md`). Event timing is ± 4 ms per edge. In close-up checks, auto toe-offs for him tended to be 0–4 frames late, so contact may read up to about 15 ms long. Contacts that tracking had split in two, or that sat at the frame edge, were corrected or dropped by hand (`events_override.yaml`).
- **Joint angles.** Where the swing knee passes the stance knee, MediaPipe's knee jumps. Those late-stance angles are blanked rather than reported, so toe-off angles have n = 5–6 at mile pace and none for the kick.
- **Braking.** This comes from differentiating the CM velocity, the noisiest metric here. SDs are about as large as the means, so don't act on it.
- **Forces and stiffness.** Spring-mass model estimates, with mass 68.9 ± 0.5 kg (the 151.8 assumed to be lb).
- **Fatigue.** He raced the day before, so these are not his fresh mechanics.
