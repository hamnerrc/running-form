# Reference: sprint mechanics for an elastic long jumper (Athlete A)

## Target
22 ft = 6.71 m. A rough projectile estimate (ours, not a published table): this needs about **8.5–9 m/s** of controlled approach speed, so ≈ 1.1 s per 10 m at 9 m/s. Speed raises the ceiling; the approach determines how much of it reaches the board.

## Why Carl Lewis, and why published data instead of a clip
Lewis is the classic sprinter-style ("elastic") long jumper. Old broadcast footage pans, has no calibrated markers, and runs at 25–30 fps (a 0.09 s contact is ~3 frames), so measuring a clip would be mostly guesswork. The studies below used high-speed cameras (250 Hz in Tokyo 1991).

## Lewis as a sprinter — Seoul 1988 100 m (9.92)
- Fastest 10 m segment: **12.04 m/s** (50–60 m).
- 30–60 m: 12.5 steps, **4.84 steps/s, 2.40 m** per step. 60–90 m: 11.3 steps, **4.41 steps/s, 2.65 m**.
- Compared with Ben Johnson, Lewis took fewer, longer steps at a lower rate. A long-stride, late-accelerating profile.
- Source: IAAF Seoul 1988 scientific project, via [Charlie Francis forum](https://forum.charliefrancis.com/t/100m-final-1988-olympics-seoul-split-times/21715)

## Lewis as a long jumper — Tokyo 1991 World Championships (8.91)
- Approach speed **11.06 m/s** (Powell 11.00; other finalists 10.20–10.58). [ISBS 2008](https://lida.sport-iat.de/twm/Record/4016739?lng=en)
- **Second-to-last stride 47 cm longer** than the one before. CM lowered 3 cm during that ground contact, then ~10 cm more during the long stride; lowest point at the last-step contact.
- Takeoff: horizontal velocity 10.5 → 9.1 m/s (−1.4), vertical +3.4 m/s, **takeoff angle 20.3°** (Powell: +4.2 m/s, 24.6°).
- The authors' reading: Lewis started converting horizontal into vertical velocity earlier, **spreading it across two strides**, essentially sprint technique adapted to jumping. Powell used a more force-based, single big takeoff. [Ariel Dynamics](https://www.arielnet.com/articles/show/adi-pub-01140/long-jump-technique-power-or-speed)
- Plant leg at touchdown: both jumpers used a leg angle just over 60°, and faster runs went with a straighter knee at touchdown. [ISBS](https://ojs.ub.uni-konstanz.de/cpa/article/view/777/700)

## Elite max-velocity norms (what Athlete A gets compared against)
- **Contact time** typically 0.08–0.10 s, shorter in faster sprinters. Elite men: contact 0.090 s, flight 0.124 s, **step length ≈ 2.39 × leg length**, step rate ≈ 4.7 /s. [PMC8580521](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8580521)
- Elite sprinters keep the **foot landing close under the body**, limit **knee bend during contact**, and **leave the ground before the hip and knee fully straighten**. [Sides](https://katalog.lib.cas.cz/KNAV/EdsRecord/edsair,edsair.core.ac.uk....405d6cd93ccc3c06b91196fb18b99332)
- Shorter contacts go with strong hip extension, a fast **backward sweep of the foot before touchdown**, and little knee flexion and extension during contact. [J Hum Kinet](https://jhk.termedia.pl/Kinematic-Stride-Characteristics-of-Maximal-Sprint-Running-of-Elite-Sprinters-Verification,158511,0,2.html)
- Thigh range of motion ≈ 100° in elite men (same PMC study).

## What this means for Athlete A
1. **Lewis is the right model for his style, but only with Lewis's speed.** At 11 m/s Lewis could take off at a flat 20°. At 8.5–9 m/s Athlete A probably needs a somewhat higher angle; one analysis estimates **~0.3 m per extra degree**. [NBC / Lees](https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna48246625). That's for the approach session.
2. **Sprint markers ranked in the baseline:** contact time as a share of each step (elastic quality, plus a stiffness estimate) · foot landing distance ahead of the hips · backward foot sweep at touchdown · toe-off before full leg extension · thigh range of motion · step length ÷ leg length (Lewis ~6'2" vs A's 5'9") · **right vs left asymmetry** (limited right dorsiflexion).
3. **Later (approach session):** the longer second-to-last stride, CM lowering across the last two steps, and the ~60° plant-leg angle. With a right-foot takeoff, the lowering step is on the **left** leg (the shin-splint side), so its loading matters.
4. **Prioritizing:** no cues that trade short contacts for "push longer". Anything that looks like a strength limit goes to the RDL / broad jump work instead.
